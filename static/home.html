How it works

Three steps, all on the kitchen laptop.

  1. 01

    Hear

    The laptop's microphone hands Kettle 10 seconds of sound at a time. It is held in memory and never written to disk.

  2. 02

    Name

    A small, frozen sound model (CED-tiny) picks one of nine activities, like cooking, washing up or TV. Unsure, or unlike anything it learned? It says "Not sure".

  3. 03

    Draw the day

    Only the activity and the time are kept. They become the day bar both screens show, and answer the checks Meera set.

What Kettle won't do

Said plainly, because a caregiver should know before trusting it.

  • It is not an emergency device. It can't detect a fall or call for help.
  • It doesn't send alerts. Meera looks when she wonders. Kettle doesn't nag.
  • It isn't proven in a real kitchen yet. Every number so far comes from one research home's recordings.
  • It doesn't learn Dad's routine. It only checks the rules Meera sets, and shows Dad what they are.

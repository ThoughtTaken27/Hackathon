# Teardown (short): Snug, Daily Safety Check-In (Sat Nov 14, 2026, Kettle stage 1, teardown agent)

https://apps.apple.com/us/app/snug-daily-safety-check-in/id1122758716 · Snug Products LLC · free tier; paid plans (a reviewer cites $20/mo) · alive (iOS update 2026-10-13) · **4.87★ from 2,273 US ratings**, the most-reviewed product in this space.

Why it's here: not a sensor product, but the largest body of reviews written by **older adults who live alone**, which is the voice the sensor products lack. 380 App Store reviews fetched (327 five-star, 13 four, 13 three, 9 two, 18 one); the 53 reviews at 1–4★ are coded in `pain-points.md`.

- **Mechanism:** one tap a day by a set time; if missed, emergency contacts are alerted (O). The opposite of passive.
- **What older adults love:** peace of mind because, living alone, they don't want an accident where nobody finds them for two or three days (4★ review, paraphrased); a daily ritual shared with a walking partner (a review left at 1★ that reads as praise).
- **What they complain about (coded):** the daily ritual itself (13 burden items: rigid one-time check-in, no snooze, quotes shown at check-in that annoy); false alarms that reach family or police (a missed check-in sent two officers; a dead phone battery triggers alerts); silent failures ("some days it texts me and some days it doesn't"); hard to cancel, keeps billing (7); "$20 a month for basic routine".
- **Lesson for Kettle:** a check-in puts the work and the failure on the parent. Kettle must ask nothing of the parent, and its "not listening" state must never be presented as "something is wrong with Dad" (Snug's dead-battery false alerts are the cautionary example).

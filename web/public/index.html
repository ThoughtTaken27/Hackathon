Privacy, by design

The sound never leaves the kitchen.

The sound model runs inside the browser on your parent's laptop. It hears ten seconds, names the activity, and forgets the sound. Only the name, the time and a few scores are sent.

Questions

Asked plainly

Does Kettle record my parent?

No. The laptop holds the last ten seconds of sound in memory, names the activity, and overwrites it. No audio is saved or sent, and no words are kept. Only labels like "Cooking, 8:05 AM" reach your phone.

What do we need?

A laptop with a microphone that can stay in the kitchen, plugged in, with Chrome, Edge or Safari open on Kettle. And your own phone or computer. No app to install, no account to create.

Does it send alerts if something is wrong?

No. Kettle is not an emergency device and can't detect falls. It shows a calm timeline and answers the checks you set, like "kitchen activity by 10 AM". You look when you wonder.

What if the laptop sleeps or the Wi-Fi drops?

Kettle keeps the screen awake while it listens. If the internet drops, it keeps labelling and sends everything when the connection is back. If the laptop is closed, your view says "Not listening", never a quiet day.

How accurate is it?

On a public benchmark it scored 84.1 macro F1 on clips it had never heard, scored once. It hasn't been measured in a real kitchen yet, and it mixes up moving about with typing most often. The "How sure" page lists every miss rate.

Can we delete everything?

Yes. Settings, Delete this home removes every label and setting from Kettle's server for good. Labels older than eight days are deleted on their own.

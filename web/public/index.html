    Why Kettle

    Why Kettle

    About 28% of Americans over 65 live alone. The people who love them mostly check in by worrying: an unanswered call, a text with no reply.

    Cameras feel like being watched. Pendants end up in drawers. So Kettle asks nothing of them, and keeps nothing it hears.

    It notes the shape of a day, cooking, eating, the TV, so a normal morning needs no anxious call, and a different one gets noticed sooner.

    Questions

    Asked plainly

    Still unsure? Watch the sample day, or set it up on two of your own devices first.

    Does Kettle record anyone?

    No. The room device holds the last ten seconds of sound in memory, names the activity, and overwrites it. No audio is saved or sent, and no words are kept. Only labels like "Cooking, 8:05 AM" reach your phone.

    What do we need?

    A spare phone, tablet or laptop that can stay in the room you choose (the kitchen, living room, bedroom, hallway or a small home), plugged in, with the screen on and Kettle open in Chrome, Edge or Safari. And your own phone or computer. No app to install, no account to create.

    Is it only for the kitchen?

    No. In setup you choose who it is for (a parent, grandparent, partner, relative, friend, or yourself) and the room: kitchen, living room, bedroom, hallway, a whole small home, or another room. Kettle uses the name you give, or simply who they are to you, like "your grandparent". Its measurements come from kitchens and one research home, so in other rooms it is a prototype: a bedroom shows an activity level, not sound names, and a bathroom is safety-only.

    Does it send alerts if something is wrong?

    Not as something we claim works. Kettle is built and measured for a calm timeline and the checks you set. The code also has an experimental layer for a few urgent sounds (a smoke alarm, screaming, a crash then a cry) that asks "Are you OK?" on the kitchen screen, but it has only been tested on public and synthetic clips, never in a home. Kettle is not an emergency device and can't call for help.

    What if the screen locks or the Wi-Fi drops?

    Kettle keeps the screen awake while it listens; phones stop the microphone if the screen locks, so it shows how to set that up. If the internet drops, it keeps labelling and sends everything when the connection is back. If the device is closed or asleep, your view says "Not listening", never a quiet day.

    How accurate is it?

    84.1 macro F1 on public benchmark clips it had never heard, scored once. It hasn't been measured in a real kitchen yet, and it most often mixes up "some activity" with typing. The "How sure" page lists every miss rate.

    Can we delete everything?

    Yes. Settings, then Delete this home, removes every label and setting from Kettle's server for good. Labels older than eight days are deleted on their own.

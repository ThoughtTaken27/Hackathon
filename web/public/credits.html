What we made this weekend

Our weekend started Friday, November 13 at 11:00 PM Central, when the briefs were released. Everything in this list was written after that.

  • The sound head

    A small classifier on top of the frozen encoder, with a "Not sure" gate for sounds it has not learned, and a second opinion that checks the encoder's own sound tags.

  • The day timeline

    Rules that turn labels into a day bar, named sound types, a "Late start" or "Quiet" check, and a heartbeat that says when the kitchen stopped listening.

  • The evaluation

    Development folds, one frozen held-out run, the other-homes test and the stress tests. Every number on How sure is produced by this code, not typed by hand.

  • Both apps

    The Python app, and the web app with its sync service, kitchen setup and family view, plus the tests. The landscapes are generated by our own script from seeded noise, not photographs. Meera and Ravi are invented people.

Data

Public datasets only. We collected no personal data and no recordings of anyone. Raw audio from these sets is not stored in the project except where a row says so.

  • SINS / DCASE 2018 Task 5, development setDekkers et al., KU Leuven ADVISE

    One person's home over a week, labelled by activity. Trains Kettle's head, runs the development folds, and supplies the sample-day demo clips (100 short audio files kept in demo_cache/, shared here with attribution as the licence asks).

    CC BY-NC 4.0
  • DCASE 2018 Task 5, evaluation audio and labelsDekkers et al., KU Leuven ADVISE

    The held-out test, run once on a seeded sample after the model was frozen. We never stored this audio. Only its label files are in the project.

    CC BY-NC-ND 4.0
  • DESED, public evaluation setTurpault, Serizel, Shah, Salamon (DCASE 2019)

    Real home videos from other people's kitchens and rooms, used only to test Kettle outside the research home. Never trained on. Audio is not stored in the project. Twenty-five clips that also appear in AudioSet, which the encoder learned from, were removed to avoid leakage. Most clips come from YouTube videos marked Creative Commons Attribution, and their uploaders are listed here. For the rest, the dataset gives no licence entry, which is another reason we store none of the audio.

    CC BY 4.0
  • Kitchen20Moreaux, Garcia Ortiz, Ferrané, Lerasle (CBMI 2019)

    Kitchen actions such as chopping, boiling water and blender, plus household sounds, for the other-homes test. Not trained on, not stored in the project.

    Code: Apache-2.0
  • ESC-50Karol Piczak (ACM Multimedia 2015)

    Sounds that are not household activities, to check that Kettle says "Not sure" instead of guessing, household sounds for the other-homes test, and clips for testing the named sound types and the privacy veto. Not stored in the project; only embeddings and scores are.

    CC BY-NC 3.0
  • AudioSet ontologyGemmeke et al., Google (ICASSP 2017)

    The 527 sound names that Kettle shows as evidence ("Dishes, pots, and pans"), and the sound classes behind the named types such as boiling, water tap and doorbell. The encoder below learned from AudioSet.

    CC BY 4.0

Pretrained models

  • CED-tinyDinkel et al., Xiaomi (arXiv 2308.11957)

    The sound encoder. It runs in the kitchen device's browser and in our Python app, frozen, so we never trained it. We exported it to ONNX ourselves, split in two because the combined file crashes the browser runtime. Its modelling code is vendored in the project, with the changes we made listed in kettle/vendor/ced/README.md.

    Apache-2.0
  • CED-small

    Comparison only, never shipped. It shows how much a bigger sibling would add.

    Apache-2.0
  • Audio Spectrogram TransformerGong, Chung, Glass (arXiv 2104.01778)

    Comparison and a zero-shot baseline, never shipped.

    BSD-3-Clause
  • LAION CLAPWu et al. (arXiv 2211.06687)

    A zero-shot baseline that matches sounds to written descriptions. Never shipped.

    Apache-2.0
  • Apple Sound RecognitionApple SoundAnalysis, built into macOS

    The baseline we compare against: what a phone can already do with no training. Used through a small command-line tool of ours, never shipped.

    Apple SDK
  • DCASE 2018 Task 5 results and official baseline

    The published scores we compare against (baseline 85.0 known-microphone and 83.1 unknown-microphone macro F1). Our held-out number is close to, not identical with, that setup, and How sure says so.

    Published results

What these licences mean for Kettle

SINS and ESC-50 are licensed for non-commercial use, so this is a research prototype. A real product would need to retrain the head on data it is allowed to sell with, such as recordings from consenting households. The sound encoder (Apache-2.0) and the code libraries carry no such limit. Kettle is a prototype, not medical advice, and not an emergency device.

The full machine-readable list, with access dates and notes, is docs/DATA-SOURCES.md in the project files.
